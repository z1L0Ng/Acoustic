\subsection{Joint Learning and Transfer}
\label{sec:protocol}
\label{sec:native_results}
Table~\ref{tab:native_results} compares LSAA with separately trained
frozen-encoder references. LSAA achieves higher means on ICBHI and both
external targets, whereas the strongest frozen references perform better
on SPRSound. The ICBHI-only and SPRSound-only controls in
Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) assess joint training within LSAA's shared
architecture.

\LSAAMainResultsTable

Providing both readouts does not make single-source training sufficient
for both datasets: each single-source model performs poorly on the other
native task. These failures accompany the within-label acoustic differences
in Figure~\ref{fig:dataset_characteristics}(b,c).
Joint supervision enables one model to serve both tasks, improving ICBHI
Score by 3.39 percentage points (pp) at a 2.13-pp cost on SPRSound relative
to their respective single-source models. Both Normal recall and correct
abnormal-subclass recall improve on ICBHI. Joint training therefore
expands task coverage, with different gains and costs relative to the
two specialist models.

External benefits depend on the source and prediction target.
LSAA's HF CAS ranking AUROC (86.44\%) is close to ICBHI-only (87.05\%)
and exceeds SPRSound-only (79.25\%), while its KAUH patient BA exceeds
both controls (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)). Combining sources thus improves
KAUH performance without producing a uniform gain across external targets.

\subsection{Supervision and Readout Analysis}
\label{sec:ablations}
\label{sec:fine_supervision}
\label{sec:readout_analysis}
\label{sec:transfer}
A binary prediction target can still benefit from fine supervision.
Retaining SPRSound C/W labels improves both native Scores and SPRSound
C/W AUROC across three seeds (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)). Coarse SPR's
mean ICBHI Score falls below ICBHI-only, showing that adding source audio
alone does not ensure a gain under this recipe. The SPRSound improvement
is uneven: Coarse includes an all-Normal seed, while the other two seeds
gain 4.57/0.15 pp. Coarse also removes C/W calibration and changes relative
loss strength, so the comparison supports the fine-supervision recipe
without isolating annotation information alone.

\LSAAAnalysisTable

Shared fine supervision does not prescribe a unique task readout.
Native + C/W improves ICBHI Score by 2.50 pp while SPRSound C/W AUROC
changes by only $-0.05$ pp. This supports separate assessment of attribute
detection and native-task performance. LSAA supplies an explicit
attribute-to-task mapping; learned native heads offer another implementation.
Because the alternative retrains both encoder and heads, the gain cannot
be attributed solely to replacing the decoder.

\label{sec:hf_supervision}
More supervision from a source need not improve its evaluation target.
HF-on lowers CAS AUROC in all three seeds ($-7.41\pm2.73$ pp), despite
improving SPRSound in two seeds; KAUH changes direction across seeds
(Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)). HF training assigns W negatives to eligible
Rhonchi/Stridor windows without Wheeze annotations, whereas CAS evaluation
treats any Wheeze/Rhonchi/Stridor annotation as positive and ranks recordings
using maximum-window Wheeze-head scores. Of 661 CAS-positive test recordings,
256 have no Wheeze annotation. Supervision and evaluation thus target
different sound categories within the same source. This mismatch may contribute
to the decline, but the current comparison does not isolate its causal effect.
