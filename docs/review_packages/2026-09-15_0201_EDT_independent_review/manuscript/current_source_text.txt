0045 | \begin{abstract}
0046 | Respiratory sound datasets provide complementary supervision, but differ
0047 | in annotation granularity and prediction targets. We propose LSAA
0048 | (Learning Shared Acoustic Attributes), a joint learning framework that
0049 | shares acoustic supervision while preserving native tasks. LSAA learns
0050 | abnormality, crackle, and wheeze from ICBHI cycles and SPRSound events,
0051 | then uses task-specific readouts for four-class cycle and binary event
0052 | prediction. This allows fine sound-type annotations to supervise learning
0053 | even when the output is binary. We further assess attribute transfer and
0054 | auxiliary supervision on HF Lung, and external generalization on KAUH.
0055 | Across three seeds with official-test-based checkpoint selection, LSAA
0056 | improves ICBHI Score by 3.39 percentage points with a 2.13-point SPRSound
0057 | decrease relative to the respective single-source models. Further
0058 | comparisons support retaining fine annotations under the evaluated
0059 | training recipe and reveal task-dependent effects of readout design and
0060 | additional supervision.
0061 | \end{abstract}

0001 | \section{Introduction}
0002 | \label{sec:introduction}
0003 | 
0004 | Automated recognition of crackles and wheezes can reduce the manual effort
0005 | involved in respiratory sound assessment\cite{hsu2021hflung}.
0006 | Training these classifiers requires expert annotations that are costly
0007 | to obtain at scale\cite{zhang2024opera}. Existing respiratory
0008 | datasets\cite{rocha2019icbhi,zhang2022sprsound} offer an opportunity to
0009 | expand supervision by reusing labeled recordings. Their annotations
0010 | describe related acoustic attributes but differ in granularity and
0011 | prediction targets, so their task labels are not directly interchangeable.
0012 | 
0013 | To reuse annotations that are not directly interchangeable, a common
0014 | strategy is to map source labels to a shared prediction target, as in
0015 | SPRSound's fusion experiments and LungMix's cross-dataset
0016 | transfer\cite{zhang2022sprsound,ge2025lungmix}. This enables reuse under an
0017 | aligned task definition, but coarsening labels can discard distinctions
0018 | needed by another task. Representing sound composition offers a more
0019 | flexible way to use label information: Multi-breath and PC-MCL employ
0020 | multilabel supervision within ICBHI\cite{chua2024multibreath,jeong2026pcmcl}.
0021 | Across datasets, DCASE combines class mappings and masked supervision while
0022 | retaining dataset-specific evaluation within a shared temporal sound-event
0023 | detection system\cite{cornell2024dcase}. These studies provide ways to align
0024 | labels and retain their information, but our setting requires sharing
0025 | supervision across distinct cycle-level and event-level prediction targets.
0026 | For example, SPRSound's Wheeze annotations contain information relevant to
0027 | ICBHI's four-class cycle task, although its binary event task requires only
0028 | normal/abnormal predictions\cite{zhang2022sprsound}. The available supervision
0029 | is therefore richer than the required output. We aim to jointly learn from
0030 | this information while preserving each dataset's native prediction target.
0031 | 
0032 | To this end, we propose LSAA (Learning Shared Acoustic Attributes).
0033 | A shared encoder learns acoustic representations through abnormality,
0034 | crackle, and wheeze supervision from ICBHI cycles and SPRSound events.
0035 | Each annotation supervises only the attributes it supports.
0036 | Task-specific readouts map the shared attribute predictions to four-class
0037 | cycle and binary event outputs, allowing fine supervision to guide
0038 | learning while preserving different native tasks.
0039 | 
0040 | In this work, we demonstrate:
0041 | \begin{enumerate}
0042 | \setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
0043 | \setlength{\parsep}{0pt}
0044 | \item We propose LSAA, a joint learning framework that connects heterogeneous
0045 | respiratory sound annotations through shared acoustic attributes in one model.
0046 | \item We develop task-specific readouts over shared abnormality, crackle,
0047 | and wheeze predictions, enabling fine-grained supervision to support
0048 | distinct native outputs.
0049 | \item We evaluate native-task performance on ICBHI and SPRSound,
0050 | investigate attribute transfer and auxiliary supervision on HF
0051 | Lung\cite{hsu2021hflung}, and assess external generalization on
0052 | KAUH\cite{fraiwan2021kauh}.
0053 | \end{enumerate}

0001 | \section{Datasets and Acoustic Characteristics}
0002 | \label{sec:data_alignment}
0003 | 
0004 | \subsection{Datasets and Annotations}
0005 | ICBHI and SPRSound provide LSAA's core joint-training data.
0006 | ICBHI annotates respiratory cycles as Normal, Crackle (C), Wheeze (W),
0007 | or Both (C and W)\cite{rocha2019icbhi}. SPRSound annotates seven event
0008 | categories, while the event task studied here predicts Normal or
0009 | Adventitious (abnormal)\cite{zhang2022sprsound}. Both datasets provide
0010 | patient identifiers and annotation boundaries. SPRSound's fine C/W labels
0011 | retain sound-type information relevant to ICBHI's four-class task, even
0012 | though its own prediction target is binary. This motivates sharing fine
0013 | acoustic supervision while retaining each task's prediction unit and target.
0014 | 
0015 | HF Lung provides timed breath-phase and adventitious-sound
0016 | annotations\cite{hsu2021hflung}. We use its source-training partition for
0017 | a separate auxiliary C/W supervision condition and its source-test
0018 | partition for attribute evaluation. KAUH provides recording-level sound
0019 | categories and Bell, Diaphragm, and Extended filter versions of each
0020 | patient's recording\cite{fraiwan2021kauh}. We use these recordings for
0021 | external evaluation, grouping the filter versions within each patient.
0022 | Figure~\ref{fig:dataset_characteristics}(a) reports original label counts
0023 | in cycles for ICBHI, events for SPRSound, annotated intervals for HF,
0024 | and filter-version recordings for KAUH.
0025 | 
0026 | \subsection{Acoustic Characteristics}
0027 | \label{sec:acoustic_characteristics}
0028 | To compare acoustic inputs across sources, we prepare mono 16-kHz,
0029 | 5-s inputs. ICBHI cycles, SPRSound events, and KAUH recordings are
0030 | repeated if shorter than 5~s or cropped to the first 5~s if longer,
0031 | with boundary fading; HF recordings supply three consecutive windows.
0032 | From globally centered 4-kHz analysis copies, we estimate 80--2000~Hz Welch
0033 | spectra\cite{welch1967spectra} and compute spectral centroid (power-weighted
0034 | mean frequency), bandwidth (frequency spread), and peak frequency.
0035 | Descriptor medians are taken within recording and then within patient;
0036 | HF uses recording-date groups. Principal component analysis (PCA) of
0037 | log-transformed, jointly standardized descriptors with equal source
0038 | weights shows source structure with partial overlap
0039 | (Figure~\ref{fig:dataset_characteristics}(b)).
0040 | 
0041 | Figure~\ref{fig:dataset_characteristics}(c) compares median spectral
0042 | centroids within Normal, Crackle, Wheeze, and Both label groups.
0043 | SPRSound's Fine/Coarse Crackle labels are combined as Crackle, and
0044 | Wheeze+Crackle corresponds to Both. ICBHI has lower
0045 | centroids than SPRSound in all four classes. HF source-test windows and
0046 | KAUH recordings with compatible labels provide additional descriptive
0047 | references. HF windows are grouped by D (discontinuous adventitious sound)
0048 | annotations without Wheeze annotations, Wheeze annotations without D,
0049 | or both annotations. Missing
0050 | annotations do not establish sound absence, and no HF Normal group is
0051 | assigned. These comparisons show that compatible sound labels accompany
0052 | differing source distributions, which shared acoustic supervision must
0053 | accommodate.

0001 | \section{Method}
0002 | \label{sec:method}
0003 | 
0004 | LSAA connects heterogeneous annotations through shared abnormality (A),
0005 | Crackle (C), and Wheeze (W) probabilities and dataset-specific readouts
0006 | (Figure~\ref{fig:method_overview}).
0007 | 
0008 | \subsection{Shared Attributes and Task Readouts}
0009 | \label{sec:annotation_alignment}
0010 | \label{sec:native_readouts}
0011 | The prediction path maps each audio unit to shared attribute probabilities
0012 | and then to the output required by its dataset. For a mono 16-kHz, 5-s input $x_i$
0013 | prepared as in Section~\ref{sec:acoustic_characteristics},
0014 | AudioSet-pretrained BEATs iter3+ AS2M\cite{chen2023beats,gemmeke2017audioset}
0015 | produces $H_i\in\mathbb{R}^{T_i\times D}$, where $T_i$ is the token count
0016 | and $D=768$.
0017 | Mean pooling over tokens followed by a linear $768\to256$ projection
0018 | gives $h_i\in\mathbb{R}^{256}$. Three parallel linear heads predict
0019 | \begin{equation}
0020 | p_{ik}=\phi_k(W_k h_i+b_k),\qquad k\in\{a,c,w\},
0021 | \label{eq:attribute_predictions}
0022 | \end{equation}
0023 | where $\phi_a$ returns the Abnormal probability from a two-logit softmax,
0024 | and $\phi_c,\phi_w$ apply sigmoid to one logit each.
0025 | Their outputs form the shared attribute vector
0026 | $\mathbf p_i=(p_{ia},p_{ic},p_{iw})$.
0027 | 
0028 | Let $\hat a_i$ be the Normal/Abnormal argmax (ties select Normal),
0029 | $\hat c_i=\mathbb{I}[p_{ic}\geq\tau_c]$, and
0030 | $\hat w_i=\mathbb{I}[p_{iw}\geq\tau_w]$.
0031 | SPRSound uses $\hat a_i$ for Normal/Adventitious classification.
0032 | For ICBHI, $\hat a_i=0$ yields Normal; otherwise, two positive attributes
0033 | yield Both and one yields its class. If neither is positive, the larger
0034 | $p_{ik}-\tau_k$ margin selects Crackle or Wheeze, with ties selecting Crackle.
0035 | Thresholds use core validation.
0036 | 
0037 | For HF source-test, maxima over C/W window probabilities give recording
0038 | scores. For KAUH, we average probabilities across each patient's filter
0039 | versions and predict Normal/Abnormal for patients with compatible sound
0040 | labels. Models and thresholds remain fixed for these evaluations.
0041 | 
0042 | \subsection{Annotation-Aware Joint Training}
0043 | \label{sec:joint_supervision}
0044 | \label{sec:hf_method}
0045 | Training uses each annotation to supervise the attributes it supports,
0046 | jointly fine-tuning the encoder and prediction heads on ICBHI and SPRSound.
0047 | Annotations supply targets $y_{ik}$ and masks $m_{ik}$,
0048 | with $m_{ik}=1$ only for supported attributes, distinguishing unknown
0049 | labels from observed negatives\cite{shi2021marginal,zhou2022unknown}.
0050 | ICBHI labels specify A/C/W. SPRSound's fine C/W labels supervise the
0051 | corresponding heads despite its binary task, while Rhonchi/Stridor
0052 | supervise A only. For batch $B$, let $B_k=\{i\in B:m_{ik}=1\}$ and
0053 | $K_B=\{k:|B_k|>0\}$. Classification uses
0054 | \begin{equation}
0055 | \mathcal L_{\rm cls}(B)=\frac{1}{|K_B|}\sum_{k\in K_B}
0056 | \frac{1}{|B_k|}\sum_{i\in B_k}\ell_k(p_{ik},y_{ik}),
0057 | \label{eq:classification_loss}
0058 | \end{equation}
0059 | where $\ell_a$ is cross-entropy on $(1-p_{ia},p_{ia})$ and
0060 | $\ell_c,\ell_w$ are binary cross-entropy.
0061 | 
0062 | Patient-based regularization uses a separate attention-based projection
0063 | $Z_i=\mathcal G(H_i)\in\mathbb{R}^{768}$ and PAFA's patient
0064 | cohesion-separation (PCSL) and global patient alignment (GPAL)
0065 | losses\cite{jeong2025pafa}. Within each core-source batch, PCSL promotes
0066 | within-patient cohesion and between-patient separation, while GPAL
0067 | limits the dispersion of patient feature means. These losses regularize
0068 | the shared encoder during joint training.
0069 | 
0070 | In the auxiliary condition, HF source-training windows add C/W supervision,
0071 | mapping the discontinuous adventitious-sound label D to C.
0072 | Their masked binary cross-entropy gives $\mathcal L_{\rm HF}$.
0073 | The treatment of derived negatives is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:hf_supervision}.
0074 | Core batches supply classification and PAFA regularization, yielding
0075 | \begin{equation}
0076 | \mathcal L=
0077 | \underbrace{\mathcal L_{\rm cls}
0078 |  +\lambda_p\mathcal L_{\rm PCSL}
0079 |  +\lambda_g\mathcal L_{\rm GPAL}}_{\mathcal L_{\rm core}}
0080 |  +\lambda_{\rm HF}\mathcal L_{\rm HF},
0081 | \label{eq:total_objective}
0082 | \end{equation}
0083 | with $\lambda_p=50$, $\lambda_g=5\times10^{-4}$, and
0084 | $\lambda_{\rm HF}=0$ for core training or $0.25$ for the auxiliary condition.
0085 | HF contributes only the C/W term, leaving A supervision and patient
0086 | regularization to the core sources.

0006 | \subsection{Joint Learning and Transfer}
0007 | \label{sec:protocol}
0008 | \label{sec:native_results}
0009 | Table~\ref{tab:native_results} compares LSAA with separately trained
0010 | frozen-encoder references. LSAA achieves higher means on ICBHI and both
0011 | external targets, whereas the strongest frozen references perform better
0012 | on SPRSound. The ICBHI-only and SPRSound-only controls in
0013 | Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a) assess joint training within LSAA's shared
0014 | architecture.
0015 | 
0016 | \LSAAMainResultsTable
0017 | 
0018 | Providing both readouts does not make single-source training sufficient
0019 | for both datasets: each single-source model performs poorly on the other
0020 | native task. These failures accompany the within-label acoustic differences
0021 | in Figure~\ref{fig:dataset_characteristics}(b,c).
0022 | Joint supervision enables one model to serve both tasks, improving ICBHI
0023 | Score by 3.39 percentage points (pp) at a 2.13-pp cost on SPRSound relative
0024 | to their respective single-source models. Both Normal recall and correct
0025 | abnormal-subclass recall improve on ICBHI. Joint training therefore
0026 | expands task coverage, with different gains and costs relative to the
0027 | two specialist models.
0028 | 
0029 | External benefits depend on the source and prediction target.
0030 | LSAA's HF CAS ranking AUROC (86.44\%) is close to ICBHI-only (87.05\%)
0031 | and exceeds SPRSound-only (79.25\%), while its KAUH patient BA exceeds
0032 | both controls (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)). Combining sources thus improves
0033 | KAUH performance without producing a uniform gain across external targets.

0035 | \subsection{Supervision and Readout Analysis}
0036 | \label{sec:ablations}
0037 | \label{sec:fine_supervision}
0038 | \label{sec:readout_analysis}
0039 | \label{sec:transfer}
0040 | A binary prediction target can still benefit from fine supervision.
0041 | Retaining SPRSound C/W labels improves both native Scores and SPRSound
0042 | C/W AUROC across three seeds (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)). Coarse SPR's
0043 | mean ICBHI Score falls below ICBHI-only, showing that adding source audio
0044 | alone does not ensure a gain under this recipe. The SPRSound improvement
0045 | is uneven: Coarse includes an all-Normal seed, while the other two seeds
0046 | gain 4.57/0.15 pp. Coarse also removes C/W calibration and changes relative
0047 | loss strength, so the comparison supports the fine-supervision recipe
0048 | without isolating annotation information alone.
0049 | 
0050 | \LSAAAnalysisTable
0051 | 
0052 | Shared fine supervision does not prescribe a unique task readout.
0053 | Native + C/W improves ICBHI Score by 2.50 pp while SPRSound C/W AUROC
0054 | changes by only $-0.05$ pp. This supports separate assessment of attribute
0055 | detection and native-task performance. LSAA supplies an explicit
0056 | attribute-to-task mapping; learned native heads offer another implementation.
0057 | Because the alternative retrains both encoder and heads, the gain cannot
0058 | be attributed solely to replacing the decoder.
0059 | 
0060 | \label{sec:hf_supervision}
0061 | More supervision from a source need not improve its evaluation target.
0062 | HF-on lowers CAS AUROC in all three seeds ($-7.41\pm2.73$ pp), despite
0063 | improving SPRSound in two seeds; KAUH changes direction across seeds
0064 | (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)). HF training assigns W negatives to eligible
0065 | Rhonchi/Stridor windows without Wheeze annotations, whereas CAS evaluation
0066 | treats any Wheeze/Rhonchi/Stridor annotation as positive and ranks recordings
0067 | using maximum-window Wheeze-head scores. Of 661 CAS-positive test recordings,
0068 | 256 have no Wheeze annotation. Supervision and evaluation thus target
0069 | different sound categories within the same source. This mismatch may contribute
0070 | to the decline, but the current comparison does not isolate its causal effect.
0071 | \par

0001 | \section{Conclusion}
0002 | \label{sec:conclusion}
0003 | We presented LSAA, a joint learning framework that connects heterogeneous
0004 | respiratory sound annotations through shared acoustic attributes.
0005 | Task-specific readouts allow one model to support cycle-level four-class
0006 | and event-level binary prediction while retaining fine-grained supervision.
0007 | Our experiments support retaining fine annotations under the evaluated
0008 | training recipe and reveal task-dependent tradeoffs from joint training
0009 | and readout design. Evaluation on HF Lung and KAUH extends this assessment
0010 | beyond the training datasets, showing that additional supervision does not
0011 | uniformly improve transfer.
