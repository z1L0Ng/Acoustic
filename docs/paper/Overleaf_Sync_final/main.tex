\begin{abstract}
Respiratory sound datasets provide complementary supervision, but differ
in annotation granularity and prediction targets. We propose LSAA
(Learning Shared Acoustic Attributes), a joint learning framework that
shares acoustic supervision while preserving native tasks. LSAA learns
abnormality, crackle, and wheeze from ICBHI cycles and SPRSound events,
then uses task-specific readouts for four-class cycle and binary event
prediction. This allows fine-grained sound-type annotations to supervise learning
even when the output is binary. We further assess attribute transfer and
auxiliary supervision on HF Lung (HF), and external generalization on KAUH.
Across three seeds with official-test-based checkpoint selection, LSAA
improves ICBHI Score by 3.39 percentage points with a 2.13-point SPRSound
decrease relative to the respective single-source models. Further
comparisons support retaining fine-grained annotations under the evaluated
training recipe and reveal task-dependent effects of readout design and
additional supervision.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
respiratory acoustics, shared supervision, heterogeneous annotations, cross-task learning

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Figure/figure2_method.pdf}
\caption{LSAA prediction and training paths.
BEATs tokens of width $D=768$ are mean-pooled and linearly projected to 256 dimensions. Parallel heads form shared abnormality (A), Crackle (C), and Wheeze (W) probabilities. Task-specific readouts convert these probabilities to dataset-specific outputs. A separate attention-based projection $\mathcal G$ supplies 768-dimensional features for the PAFA patient regularizers. ICBHI and SPRSound provide core attribute supervision and patient groups;
the optional HF term uses only source-train C/W supervision. HF source-test uses maximum-window Wheeze-head scores for continuous adventitious sounds (CAS) ranking, with Wheeze/Rhonchi/Stridor forming the
positive class. Models and thresholds remain fixed for HF and KAUH evaluation. See Section~\ref{sec:method}.}
\label{fig:method_overview}
\end{figure*}
