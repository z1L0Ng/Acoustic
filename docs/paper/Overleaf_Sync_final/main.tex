\begin{abstract}
Respiratory sound datasets annotate related events at different granularities.
We study whether shared fine-attribute supervision helps native tasks and
whether its benefits depend on the classification interface. A shared BEATs
encoder learns abnormality, crackle, and wheeze from ICBHI cycles and SPRSound
events. HF supports a separate auxiliary-supervision study and attribute
evaluation; KAUH provides external evaluation. Within-class acoustic
differences persist between ICBHI and SPRSound after common 5-s preparation.
We compare fine and coarse supervision and two classification interfaces
across three paired seeds using official-test-based checkpoint selection.
Retaining fine annotations produces higher native-task Scores and C/W macro
AUROC than coarse supervision, whose performance varies sharply across seeds.
Native heads with matched C/W supervision improve ICBHI Score by 2.50
percentage points on average over the two-level reference, while SPRSound
Score changes direction across seeds.
These observations support reusing fine annotations while assessing native
readout choices and task-specific costs separately.
\end{abstract}

respiratory sounds, shared supervision, heterogeneous annotations, acoustic attributes, hierarchical decoding
