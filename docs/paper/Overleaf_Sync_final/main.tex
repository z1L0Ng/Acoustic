\begin{abstract}
Public respiratory sound datasets carry different levels of annotation detail and are labeled for different classification or prediction tasks. We propose \textsc{LSAA} (\underline{L}earning \underline{S}hared \underline{A}coustic \underline{A}ttributes), a joint learning framework that shares acoustic supervision while preserving native tasks. \textsc{LSAA} jointly learns abnormality, crackle, and wheeze from the International Conference on Biomedical and Health Informatics (ICBHI) dataset cycles and Open-Source SJTU Paediatric Respiratory Sound (SPRSound) dataset events, then uses task-specific readouts for each dataset. This allows fine-grained sound-type annotations to supervise learning even when the output is binary. We evaluate fixed-model transfer on HF Lung (HF) test recordings and KAUH. Across three seeds, \textsc{LSAA} improves ICBHI Score by 3.39 percentage points with a 2.12 pp SPRSound decrease relative to the respective single-source models. Further comparisons support retaining fine grained annotations under the evaluated training setups and reveal task-dependent effects of readout design and additional supervision.
\end{abstract}
