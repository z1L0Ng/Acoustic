\begin{abstract}
\revisiondone\ Respiratory sound datasets contain complementary annotations, but differences
in granularity and task definitions hinder their joint use. We propose CHORD
(Cross-task Harmonization Of Respiratory Datasets), an annotation-aware
framework that shares acoustic supervision while retaining native tasks.
A shared encoder jointly learns abnormality, crackle (C), and wheeze (W)
from ICBHI cycles and SPRSound events, using supported labels to supervise
each attribute. Task readouts recover four-class cycle and binary event
predictions. HF Lung supports separate auxiliary supervision and attribute
evaluation; KAUH provides external evaluation.
Across three seeds with official-test-based checkpoint selection, CHORD
improves ICBHI Score by 3.39 percentage points and reduces SPRSound Score
by 2.13 points relative to their respective single-source models.
Retaining fine labels benefits the binary task under the evaluated loss
weighting, while native classifiers can improve task accuracy at similar
C/W discrimination. Added HF supervision has task-dependent effects.
These findings support sharing fine acoustic supervision while assessing
native readouts and transfer separately.
\end{abstract}

\begin{keywords}
respiratory sounds, shared supervision, heterogeneous annotations, acoustic attributes, cross-task learning

(c,d) Within-class ICBHI/SPRSound comparisons of signal level and spectral
centroid, respectively. Points denote source medians; bars show 95\%
confidence intervals from 1,000 patient bootstraps.
Descriptors use globally centered 5-s inputs; level is uncalibrated dBFS.
Spectral measures use 80--2000~Hz Welch power
(4-kHz analysis copies, 1-s Hann windows, 50\% overlap).}
