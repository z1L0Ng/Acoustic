\subsection{Joint Learning and Transfer}
\label{sec:protocol}
\label{sec:native_results}
Table~\ref{tab:native_results} compares LSAA with separately trained fro\-zen-en\-coder references. Frozen BEATs nearly matches SPRSound-only (92.4\% versus 92.8\%), whereas ICBHI-only reaches 57.8\% compared with 51.7\% for frozen BEATs (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)). This smaller
observed gap suggests less performance headroom on SPRSound within the tested configurations, providing context for the uneven gains from joint training.

\LSAAMainResultsTable

Single-source training gives poor performance on the other native task despite access to both readouts, alongside the within-label acoustic differences in Figure~\ref{fig:dataset_characteristics}(b,c). Joint supervision enables one model to serve both tasks, improving ICBHI Score by 3.39 percentage points (pp) at a 2.13-pp cost on SPRSound relative to their respective single-source models. ICBHI's Normal recall ($Sp$) increases from 72.8\% to 75.0\%, and correct abnormal-subclass recall ($Se$) from 42.8\% to 47.4\%. Joint training thus supports both native tasks with tradeoffs relative to the single-source models.

External benefits depend on the source and prediction target. LSAA's HF CAS ranking AUROC (86.44\%) is close to ICBHI-only (87.05\%) and exceeds SPRSound-only (79.25\%), while its KAUH patient BA exceeds both controls (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(a)). KAUH's abnormal-class spectral centroids are closer to ICBHI than to SPRSound (Figure~\ref{fig:dataset_characteristics}(c)), consistent with stronger KAUH transfer from ICBHI-only. Joint training improves KAUH performance, but its external gains depend on the evaluation target.

\label{sec:ablations}

\label{sec:transfer}
A binary prediction target can still benefit from fine-grained supervision. Retaining SPRSound C/W labels improves both native Scores and SPRSound C/W macro AUROC across three seeds (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(b)). Coarse SPR's
mean ICBHI Score falls below ICBHI-only, showing that adding source audio alone does not ensure a gain under this recipe. The SPRSound improvement is uneven: Coarse SPR includes an all-Normal seed, while the other two seeds
gain 4.57/0.15 pp.

\LSAAAnalysisTable

Shared fine-grained supervision does not prescribe a unique readout. Native + C/W improves ICBHI Score by 2.50 pp while SPRSound C/W macro AUROC changes by only $-0.05$ pp. This supports separate assessment of attribute detection and native-task performance. LSAA explicitly maps attributes to tasks; native heads remain an alternative.

\label{sec:hf_supervision}
More supervision from a source need not improve its evaluation target. HF-on reduces ICBHI Score by 1.90 pp on average and CAS AUROC in all three seeds ($-7.41\pm2.73$ pp), despite improving SPRSound in two seeds; KAUH changes direction across seeds (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}(c)). CAS evaluation uses a broader positive class than the auxiliary Wheeze target, so additional Wheeze supervision need not improve CAS ranking. Of 661 CAS-positive test recordings, 256 have no Wheeze annotation. Supervision and evaluation thus target different sound categories within the same source. This mismatch may contribute
to the decline, but the current comparison does not isolate its causal effect.
