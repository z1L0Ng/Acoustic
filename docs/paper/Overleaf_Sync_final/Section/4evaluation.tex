\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}

We evaluate whether shared attribute supervision supports both native
classification tasks, how additional HF supervision changes performance,
and which effects arise from supervision information or classification
design. Tables~\ref{tab:native_results}--\ref{tab:ablations} address these
questions while retaining the four dataset roles in Section~\ref{sec:data_alignment}.

\subsection{Experimental Protocol and Metrics}
\label{sec:protocol}
The benchmark model uses the 5-s inputs and losses in Section~\ref{sec:method},
batch size 32, Adam with learning rate $5\times10^{-5}$ and weight decay
$10^{-6}$, a 50-epoch cosine schedule, and parameter EMA 0.5. Each joint
training epoch contains 326 updates, equally divided between ICBHI and SPRSound.

For our benchmark in Table~\ref{tab:native_results}, checkpoints and
patience-10 early stopping use ICBHI official-test Score. This follows the
test-based selection approach in the released PAFA, SG-SCL, and Patch-Mix
implementations\cite{jeong2025pafa,kim2024sgscl,bae2023patchmix}.
C/W thresholds use internal validation data; SPRSound inter is evaluated
once per selected checkpoint. We report three completed runs with seeds
0, 1, and 42, using their mean and sample standard deviation.

Table~\ref{tab:ablations} uses seed 42 throughout and reuses the
corresponding Full model from Table~\ref{tab:native_results}. Four new
conditions start from the same pretrained BEATs initialization.
They retain its patient-grouped subtraining/validation splits:
3,174/968 ICBHI cycles from 63/16 patients, and 5,219/1,437 SPRSound
events from 194/49 patients.

At each epoch, C/W thresholds are fit to eligible annotations in the
training sources' validation data before test scoring.
Full, ICBHI-only, Coarse SPR, and Native + attributes select checkpoints
using ICBHI official-test Score; SPRSound-only uses its own official
inter-subject Score. The same criterion drives patience-10 early stopping,
with strict improvement and earlier-epoch tie-breaking.
Single-source models evaluate the other dataset only after the source-selected
checkpoint and thresholds are frozen; that target supplies no training,
calibration, or selection information.

All conditions allow 50 epochs of 326 updates, using the original
epoch-wise cosine schedule with a constant learning rate within each epoch.
Joint training assigns 163 updates to each source, whereas single-source
training assigns all 326 to its source. Thus, maximum total updates match,
but each source receives twice as many updates per epoch in single-source
training. These are single-run benchmark controls; their selection objectives
and source exposure must be considered when interpreting differences.

ICBHI Score is $(Sp+Se)/2$, where $Sp$ is Normal recall and $Se$ counts
abnormal cycles assigned to the correct abnormal subclass.
SPRSound Task1-1 uses the official inter-subject test and
Score $=(AS+HS)/2$, with $AS=(Se+Sp)/2$ and $HS=2SeSp/(Se+Sp)$;
here $Se$ and $Sp$ are Adventitious and Normal recall.
Macro-F1 averages the class-wise F1 values.
C/W macro AUROC is the mean of the two attribute AUROCs, each computed
on SPRSound inter events eligible for that target. AUPRC denotes the area
under the precision--recall curve.
On the HF source-test partition, interval recall counts a labeled D/W
interval when any overlapping 5-s window reaches the fixed attribute
threshold, over 1,812 D and 1,430 Wheeze intervals.
Recording AUROC uses maximum scores across the three windows
on the 957 recordings with at least one D/W/R/S annotation; absent target
annotations supply derived negatives. KAUH averages B/D/E probabilities
within patient before reporting binary balanced accuracy (BA), four-class
Macro-F1, and unweighted average recall (UAR) on the 86 compatible patients.

\subsection{Benchmark Results and Comparison}
\label{sec:native_results}
Table~\ref{tab:native_results} compares our joint model with published
ICBHI-only methods. Our model achieves ICBHI Score $61.17\pm0.31\%$ and
SPRSound official Score $90.70\pm0.34\%$.
Relative to PAFA, our mean $Se$ differs by only 0.28 percentage points (pp)
($47.35\%$ versus $47.63\%$), whereas $Sp$ is 7.07 pp lower
($74.98\%$ versus $82.05\%$). The ICBHI Score difference is therefore
concentrated in specificity. Our ICBHI Score is 3.67 pp below PAFA and
4.20 pp below PC-MCL. Table~\ref{tab:ablations} examines native-task
trade-offs with single-source benchmark controls.

Niizumi et al.\cite{niizumi2025foundationeval} evaluated frozen audio
encoders with trainable task networks, reporting performance differences
across tasks and head architectures. The lower block of
Table~\ref{tab:native_results} reports single-dataset frozen-encoder
references with the same 5-s waveform preparation as our method.
Values are mean $\pm$ sample SD over seeds 0, 1, and 42.
Frozen BEATs reaches $51.68\pm1.19\%$ ICBHI Score and
$92.37\pm0.35\%$ SPRSound inter Score.
These references use ICBHI official-test selection and separate checkpoint
selection for SPRSound intra/inter evaluation. Their frozen, single-dataset
training differs from our joint full fine-tuning.

\textit{Fixed readout analysis.}
Using the three ICBHI-test-selected benchmark checkpoints, Direct C/W
thresholding replaces the ICBHI two-level rule with decoding from C/W
presence alone, keeping scores and thresholds fixed. Neither attribute gives
Normal, one gives its class, and two give Both. PC-MCL adds an explicit
Normal target during multi-cycle training but also uses C/W-based class
conversion at inference\cite{jeong2026pcmcl}.
ICBHI Score becomes $60.29\pm1.81\%$, a paired change of
$-0.88\pm1.60$ pp; $Sp$ decreases by 6.80 pp and $Se$ increases by
5.04 pp. The Score differences for seeds 0/1/42 are
$-0.60/-2.60/+0.56$ pp, while Both recall is unchanged in these runs.

\textit{Attribute prediction.}
The same checkpoints achieve Crackle/Wheeze AUROCs of
$96.08\pm0.41\%$/$98.56\pm0.11\%$ and AUPRCs of
$60.32\pm3.86\%$/$94.51\pm0.56\%$ on all 1,429 SPRSound inter events.
The C/W macro AUROC is $97.32\pm0.15\%$, averaging attributes within
each run before computing the mean and sample SD across runs.

\textit{External evaluation.}
These same three models, with core-validation thresholds frozen, attain
HF D/Wheeze recording AUROCs of $67.82\pm4.42\%$/$86.36\pm1.38\%$
and interval recalls of $58.28\pm12.63\%$/$85.43\pm1.07\%$.
On KAUH, patient-level binary BA is $72.17\pm1.64\%$, four-class
Macro-F1 is $33.83\pm3.17\%$, and UAR is $50.24\pm6.10\%$.

\textit{HF supervision in an independent seed 42 pair.}
A separate HF-off/on pair uses ICBHI-test-based checkpoint selection
and core-validation thresholds. HF-on includes the auxiliary objective
in Section~\ref{sec:hf_method}; its own HF-off model supplies the reference.
ICBHI Score changes from 60.05\% to 59.71\%, while SPRSound Score changes
from 89.20\% to 91.81\%; ICBHI $Se$ falls from 45.37\% to 39.25\%.
SPRSound C/W macro AUROC changes from 96.81\% to 96.95\%,
while macro AUPRC changes from 76.24\% to 75.12\%.
HF D/Wheeze recording AUROCs change from 50.81\%/85.50\% to
69.80\%/89.83\%. D interval recall rises from 52.65\% to 93.10\%,
whereas Wheeze interval recall falls from 93.78\% to 76.92\%.
On KAUH, patient-level binary BA falls from 74.76\% to 70.14\%,
four-class Macro-F1 from 51.47\% to 36.65\%, and UAR from 62.33\% to
38.39\%. This pair shows gains on HF recording discrimination alongside
costs in native subclass recall and external classification.
The additional HF batches also increase computation.

and classification interfaces against the reused Full seed-42 reference.
The four controls retain its input processing, pretrained
encoder, PAFA regularization, optimizer, and maximum update budget.

Every training batch of the reused Full model contains eligible A/C/W
targets, so Equation~\ref{eq:classification_loss} gives each node weight
$1/3$. The hierarchy controls explicitly use $(L_A+L_C+L_W)/3$.
Each $L_k$ averages eligible rows and is zero for an empty node; missing
terms do not redistribute their weights. Coarse SPR retains its audio
and binary A supervision but withholds SPR C/W annotations from training,
calibration, selection, and early stopping. Its SPR batches therefore
use $L_A/3$, with PAFA unchanged; C/W thresholds use ICBHI validation only.
Withheld SPR attributes are read only for terminal inter-set AUROC evaluation.

\textit{Source sharing.}
Full scores 5.00 pp above ICBHI-only on ICBHI:
$Sp$ increases from 67.13\% to 80.37\%, while $Se$ decreases from
45.20\% to 41.97\%. On SPRSound, Full scores 1.95 pp below SPRSound-only,
with C/W macro AUROC 0.69 pp lower.
Fixed transfer gives 56.86\% SPR Score from ICBHI-only and
47.93\% ICBHI Score from SPRSound-only.
The SPRSound-only contrast also changes the selection objective;
both single-source conditions change source exposure. These differences
therefore cannot be attributed entirely to source sharing.

\textit{Annotation information.}
At seed 42, withholding SPR C/W targets while retaining its audio and
abnormality labels lowers SPR C/W macro AUROC by 3.50 pp, ICBHI Score by
4.46 pp, and SPRSound Score by 0.15 pp relative to Full.
The retained loss weights remain fixed, and withheld fine labels are used
only for evaluation. Full therefore has stronger attribute discrimination
and a higher ICBHI Score than Coarse SPR in this single-seed benchmark.

\textit{Classification design.}
Native + attributes uses linear $256\!\to\!4$ ICBHI and $256\!\to\!2$
SPRSound heads, plus two shared linear $256\!\to\!1$ C/W heads,
with no separate A head. On a source-homogeneous batch, its classification loss is
$(L_{\rm native}+L_C+L_W)/3$: $L_{\rm native}$ is the source's softmax CE,
and C/W targets, masks, eligible-row averaging, and coefficients match Full.
PAFA is unchanged. Native-head argmax drives selection and native-task
prediction; C/W scores are retained for attribute AUROC, without entering
native decisions. This comparison changes the native objective and head
capacity while matching C/W supervision. Relative to Full at seed 42,
Native + attributes increases ICBHI/SPRSound Scores by 1.13/1.58 pp
and SPR C/W macro AUROC by 0.24 pp. It is higher than Full on all three metrics
in this single-seed benchmark.
