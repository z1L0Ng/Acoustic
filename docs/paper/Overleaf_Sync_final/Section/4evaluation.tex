\section{Evaluation}
\vspace{-3mm}
\label{sec:evaluation}
In the tables, LSAA denotes the reference configuration with native task heads and shared C/W supervision. Results are percentages, reported as three-seed means with subscripted sample standard deviations. Bold and underlining indicate the highest and second-highest means where used.

For ICBHI cycles, Score is $(Sp+Se)/2$, where specificity $Sp$ is Normal recall and sensitivity $Se$ is the fraction of all abnormal cycles assigned their correct Crackle, Wheeze, or Both class\cite{rocha2019icbhi}. For SPRSound events, Score is $(AS+HS)/2$, where average score $AS=(r_N+r_A)/2$ and harmonic score $HS=2r_Nr_A/(r_N+r_A)$ use Normal recall $r_N$ and Adventitious recall $r_A$; $HS=0$ when both recalls are zero\cite{zhang2022sprsound}. HF uses recording-level area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUROC): recordings with Wheeze, Rhonchi, or Stridor annotations are positive, and other eligible recordings form the Other class, which does not denote Normal. We evaluate 957 HF test recordings with adventitious-sound annotations (661 positive and 296 Other); phase-only and unannotated recordings are excluded. KAUH uses patient-level balanced accuracy (BA), the mean Normal/Abnormal recall after aggregating filter-version probabilities.

\subsection{Joint Learning and Transfer}
\vspace{-2mm}
\label{sec:protocol}
\label{sec:native_results}
One joint model supports both native tasks with competitive transfer performance (Table~\ref{tab:native_results}). LSAA exceeds every frozen-encoder reference in mean ICBHI Score, HF AUROC, and KAUH BA, while its SPRSound Score of 90.86\% is 0.69 percentage points (pp) below the strongest reference, BEATs. Encoder fine-tuning also differs from the frozen references~\cite{niizumi2025foundationeval}, so these gains reflect the complete system; the ablations examine shared supervision more directly.

\LSAAMainResultsTable

The multilabel adaptations show that advantages depend on the evaluation target (Table~\ref{tab:system_comparison}). LSAA has the highest mean ICBHI Score, HF AUROC, and KAUH BA, whereas DCASE leads on SPRSound. DCASE shares a union of native classes, while LSAA combines shared C/W supervision with separate native task heads. However, DCASE uses frozen BEATs with a trainable convolutional recurrent network, while LSAA fine-tunes the encoder. PC-MCL trains only on ICBHI, making its SPRSound result a cross-source comparison. These system differences prevent attributing the rankings to label representation alone.

\subsection{Supervision and Transfer Analysis}
\vspace{-2mm}
\label{sec:ablations}
\label{sec:transfer}
The single-source models have limited cross-dataset task coverage (Table~\ref{tab:ablations}). LSAA-I (ICBHI only) achieves a SPRSound Score of 49.53\%, while LSAA-S (SPRSound only) achieves an ICBHI Score of 47.75\%. Their limited cross-source performance motivates sharing supervision across datasets, addressing the transfer question raised by Figure~\ref{fig:dataset_characteristics}(b,c).

\LSAAAnalysisTable

\label{sec:fine_supervision}
\label{sec:readout_analysis}
Shared C/W supervision supports native prediction and external transfer. LSAA w/o C+W retains the native task heads but removes the auxiliary C/W losses. Compared with this variant, LSAA improves mean SPRSound Score by 1.93 pp, HF AUROC by 7.16 pp, and KAUH BA by 2.32 pp, with a 0.20 pp ICBHI Score decrease. Fine-grained labels can therefore support a binary native task and external transfer while largely preserving ICBHI performance.

Native and transfer performance show different rankings in the PAFA comparison. LSAA has a 5.07 pp higher ICBHI Score than LSAA w/o PAFA, while its KAUH BA is 0.39 pp lower. Higher native-task performance does not necessarily coincide with stronger external transfer.

\label{sec:hf_supervision}
Additional HF supervision does not yield uniformly stronger performance. Relative to LSAA, LSAA w/HF has a 0.76 pp higher mean SPRSound Score, but lower ICBHI Score, HF AUROC, and KAUH BA by 4.78, 8.47, and 2.40 pp, respectively. Its Wheeze supervision is narrower than the HF evaluation target: 256 of 661 positive recordings lack Wheeze annotations. Supervision and evaluation from the same dataset can therefore target different sound categories; additional labels must be assessed against the prediction target.
