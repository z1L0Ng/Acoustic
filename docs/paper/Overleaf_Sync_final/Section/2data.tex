\section{Datasets and Acoustic Characteristics}
\label{sec:data_alignment}\label{sec:acoustic_characteristics}
\noindent\textbf{Datasets and Annotations. }
ICBHI~\cite{rocha2019icbhi} and SPRSound (SPR in tables)~\cite{zhang2022sprsound} contain recordings from 126 and 292 participants, respectively, with different annotation units and label details. As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset_characteristics}(a), ICBHI labels respiratory cycles as Normal (\texttt{N}), Crackle (\texttt{C}), Wheeze (\texttt{W}), or Both (\texttt{C}-\texttt{W}). SPRSound provides seven event categories. Both datasets provide patient identifiers and annotation boundaries. HF~\cite{hsu2021hflung} contains recordings from 279 participants and annotates Inhalation, Exhalation, Discontinuous adventitious sounds, and Continuous adventitious sounds (CAS: Wheeze, Rhonchi, Stridor). KAUH~\cite{fraiwan2021kauh} contains 112 participants, with Bell, Diaphragm, and Extended filter versions of each recording. The original labels include Normal, Crackles, Inspiratory Crackles, Expiratory Wheezes, Inspiratory and Expiratory Wheezes, Inspiratory Crackles with Expiratory Wheezes, alongside crepitations and bronchial sounds.

\noindent\textbf{Label Mapping. }
We retain ICBHI's four native classes and use SPRSound's event-level binary task (Task 1-1), which distinguishes Normal from Adventitious (abnormal)~\cite{zhang2022sprsound}. We merged SPRSound's Fine and Coarse Crackle labels into Crackle (\texttt{C}) and mapped Wheeze+Crackle to Both (\texttt{C}-\texttt{W}). For HF, we mapped Discontinuous adventitious sounds (D in HF lables) to Crackle (\texttt{C}) while keeping Wheeze (\texttt{W)}. For KAUH, we mapped N, C/IC, EW/IEW, and ICEW to Normal (\texttt{N}), Crackle (\texttt{C}), Wheeze (\texttt{W}), and Both (\texttt{C}-\texttt{W}), respectively, and excluded all other labels. This yields 86 participants (258 filtered files) for binary external evaluation. 

\noindent\textbf{Acoustic Characteristics. }
%The shared C/W labels in ICBHI and SPRSound provide a basis for joint supervision. 
We use mono 16~kHz, 5 seconds input with repeat padding or front cropping, boundary fading, or consecutive windows for each sample and then produce spectral centroid, bandwidth, and peak frequency from Welch spectra. Descriptors are aggregated by median within recording and then within patient (recording date for HF). We also conduct a log transformation and joint standardization for principal component analysis (PCA). 

As shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset_characteristics}(a), Normal is the largest category in both ICBHI and SPRSound datasets. Moreover, the two datasets partially overlap in the PCA projection (Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset_characteristics}(c)). Hence, we compared corresponding sound labels to examine whether the differences persist within sound categories. Fig.~\ref{fig:dataset_characteristics}(b) also shows lower median spectral centroids for ICBHI than SPRSound in all four corresponding groups.% HF groups reflect D/W annotations, not confirmed sound absence, so no Normal group is assigned; KAUH codes N, C/IC, EW/IEW, and ICEW map to Normal, Crackle, Wheeze, and Both, with other codes excluded. These observations motivate the joint-training and transfer comparisons in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation}.
