\section{Datasets and Acoustic Characteristics}
\label{sec:data_alignment}

\subsection{Datasets and Annotations}
ICBHI and SPRSound provide LSAA's core joint-training data.
ICBHI annotates respiratory cycles as Normal, Crackle (C), Wheeze (W),
or Both (C and W)\cite{rocha2019icbhi}. SPRSound annotates seven event
categories, while the event task studied here predicts Normal or
Adventitious (abnormal)\cite{zhang2022sprsound}. Both datasets provide
patient identifiers and annotation boundaries. SPRSound's fine C/W labels
retain sound-type information relevant to ICBHI's four-class task, even
though its own prediction target is binary. This motivates sharing fine
acoustic supervision while retaining each task's prediction unit and target.

HF Lung provides timed breath-phase and adventitious-sound
annotations\cite{hsu2021hflung}. We use its source-training partition for
a separate auxiliary C/W supervision condition and its source-test
partition for attribute evaluation. KAUH provides recording-level sound
categories and Bell, Diaphragm, and Extended filter versions of each
patient's recording\cite{fraiwan2021kauh}. We use these recordings for
external evaluation, grouping the filter versions within each patient.
Figure~\ref{fig:dataset_characteristics}(a) reports original label counts
in cycles for ICBHI, events for SPRSound, annotated intervals for HF,
and filter-version recordings for KAUH.

\subsection{Acoustic Characteristics}
\label{sec:acoustic_characteristics}
To compare acoustic inputs across sources, we prepare mono 16-kHz,
5-s inputs. ICBHI cycles, SPRSound events, and KAUH recordings are
repeated if shorter than 5~s or cropped to the first 5~s if longer,
with boundary fading; HF recordings supply three consecutive windows.
From globally centered 4-kHz analysis copies, we estimate 80--2000~Hz Welch
spectra\cite{welch1967spectra} and compute spectral centroid (power-weighted
mean frequency), bandwidth (frequency spread), and peak frequency.
Descriptor medians are taken within recording and then within patient;
HF uses recording-date groups. Principal component analysis (PCA) of
log-transformed, jointly standardized descriptors with equal source
weights shows source structure with partial overlap
(Figure~\ref{fig:dataset_characteristics}(b)).

Figure~\ref{fig:dataset_characteristics}(c) compares median spectral
centroids within Normal, Crackle, Wheeze, and Both label groups.
SPRSound's Fine/Coarse Crackle labels are combined as Crackle, and
Wheeze+Crackle corresponds to Both. ICBHI has lower
centroids than SPRSound in all four classes. HF source-test windows and
KAUH recordings with compatible labels provide additional descriptive
references. HF windows are grouped by D (discontinuous adventitious sound)
annotations without Wheeze annotations, Wheeze annotations without D,
or both annotations. Missing
annotations do not establish sound absence, and no HF Normal group is
assigned. These comparisons show that compatible sound labels accompany
differing source distributions, which shared acoustic supervision must
accommodate.
