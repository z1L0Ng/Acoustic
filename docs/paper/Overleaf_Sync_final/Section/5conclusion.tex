\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We studied shared respiratory attributes while retaining dataset-native
tasks. Acoustic differences persisted within compatible labels after common
input preparation. Across three paired seeds, retaining fine SPRSound
annotations improved both native-task Scores and attribute discrimination
over coarse supervision, whose performance varied strongly across seeds.
Native heads with matched C/W supervision consistently improved ICBHI Score
over the two-level reference, while SPRSound changes varied in sign.
Source comparisons and the separate HF-supervision case showed further
task-dependent gains and costs. These findings support reusing available
fine annotations and assessing classification choices separately under the
studied corpora and selection protocols.
