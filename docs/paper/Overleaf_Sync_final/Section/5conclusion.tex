\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented LSAA, a joint learning framework that connects heterogeneous respiratory sound annotations through shared acoustic attributes. Task-specific readouts allow one model to support cycle-level four-class and event-level binary prediction while retaining fine-grained supervision. Our experiments support retaining fine-grained annotations under the evaluated training recipe and reveal task-dependent tradeoffs from joint training and readout design. Evaluation on HF and KAUH extends this assessment beyond the training datasets, showing that more supervision need not improve transfer.
