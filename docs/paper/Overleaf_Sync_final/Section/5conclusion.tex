\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
\vspace{2mm}
In this work, we presented LSAA, which combines shared acoustic attribute supervision with dataset-specific native heads to learn from heterogeneous respiratory sound annotations. Shared \texttt{C-W} supervision improves mean SPRSound performance and transfer to HF and KAUH, with a small ICBHI performance decrease relative to native-task training alone. These results suggest that fine-grained annotations can support learning beyond the labels required by a native task. Future work will explore a broader set of shared acoustic attributes, as well as more effective ways to combine and weight their supervision to better support the characteristics of each downstream task.
