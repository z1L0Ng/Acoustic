\title{Shared Attribute Supervision for Respiratory Sound Learning Across Native Tasks}
\author{}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Respiratory-sound datasets annotate related acoustic phenomena using different
units, category granularities, and annotation coverage. We investigate whether
compatible supervision from these datasets can support shared sound attributes
while retaining their native classification targets. A shared BEATs encoder
learns abnormality, Crackle, and Wheeze predictions from annotation-supported
targets. A two-level decision scheme uses these predictions for ICBHI
cycle-level four-class classification and SPRSound event-level
Normal/Adventitious classification. The study distinguishes the reuse of
attribute information across annotation conditions from the effect of the
final task readout. Preliminary results from three ICBHI-test-selected runs
achieve an ICBHI Score of $61.17\pm0.31\%$ and a SPRSound official Score of
$90.70\pm0.34\%$ with one joint model per run. These results establish a
native-task operating point. Whether cross-source supervision compensates for
missing fine-grained labels, and how much the two-level decision contributes,
remain to be determined by the specified matched ablations.
\end{abstract}
