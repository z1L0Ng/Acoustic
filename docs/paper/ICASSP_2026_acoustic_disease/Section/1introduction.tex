\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}
Respiratory-sound datasets provide complementary observations of related
acoustic events, but their annotations answer different questions.
ICBHI assigns Normal, Crackle, Wheeze, or Both to complete respiratory
cycles. SPRSound annotates individual sound events and defines both coarse
Normal/Adventitious classification and finer event categories
\cite{rocha2019icbhi,zhang2022sprsound}. Differences in annotation units,
category granularity, and coverage therefore accompany the acoustic
variation across data sources. Using these datasets together requires deciding
which supervision can be shared and which distinctions each task must retain.

A shared acoustic representation offers a way to connect these tasks.
Crackle and Wheeze annotations can supervise the same attributes across
sources, while an abnormality label supplies a coarser distinction.
However, these training signals carry different amounts of information.
An abnormality label alone does not specify a fine sound attribute, and
merging fine categories can remove distinctions required by another task.
The central question is whether compatible attribute supervision can preserve
useful acoustic information across these annotation conditions and support
the original classification targets through a common model.

Several lines of work provide the foundations for this question.
SPRSound includes an ICBHI/SPRSound data-fusion benchmark
\cite{zhang2022sprsound}, and LungMix unifies respiratory labels into a common
four-class space for single-source generalization\cite{ge2025lungmix}.
OPERA learns shared respiratory representations and evaluates them with
separate downstream predictors\cite{zhang2024opera}.
In heterogeneous supervised learning, universal taxonomies map shared
predictions back to dataset-specific labels\cite{bevandic2022overlapping},
and DCASE~2024 combines audio datasets with label mapping and missing-target
masking\cite{cornell2024dcase}. Respiratory attribute classification also has
close precedents: PC-MCL uses explicit multi-label targets and deterministic
four-class conversion\cite{jeong2026pcmcl}, while OCAD develops event factors
and structured compositional decoding\cite{zhang2026ocad}.
Our focus is the specific relationship between shared attribute supervision,
annotation conditions, and native respiratory-task readouts.

We study joint ICBHI cycle-four-class and SPRSound event-binary learning.
The model shares abnormality, Crackle, and Wheeze predictions, supervises each
only where the source annotation supports a target, and applies a two-level
decision scheme. SPRSound reads abnormality directly; ICBHI combines it with
the two attributes. This separates the supervision available during learning
from the output requested by a task. In particular, a binary endpoint can
still use fine labels available in its training data.

The draft is organized around three contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item An annotation-aware joint learning framework that shares respiratory
attributes across cycle and event annotations and returns their native
classification targets through a two-level decision scheme.
\item A characterization of cross-source attribute reuse under coarse and
partially available annotations, examining whether another source's
supervision supports fine-grained recognition.
\item A supervision-matched analysis of the separate effects of attribute
information and task decoding on native-task performance and class-specific
trade-offs.
\end{enumerate}
