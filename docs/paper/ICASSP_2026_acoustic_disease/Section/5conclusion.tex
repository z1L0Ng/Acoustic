\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We study shared respiratory-sound attribute supervision as a link between
heterogeneous annotations and native classification tasks. The framework
uses supported abnormality and C/W targets to train one acoustic model,
then returns ICBHI cycle-four-class and SPRSound event-binary predictions
through a two-level decision scheme. Existing results demonstrate this
joint prediction capability and identify uneven fine-category recognition.
The remaining empirical questions concern how attribute information transfers
when fine labels are unavailable and what the decision hierarchy adds at
matched supervision. The specified ablations will determine these effects
and the resulting scope of the empirical contributions.
