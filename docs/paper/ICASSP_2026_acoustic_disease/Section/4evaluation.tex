\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}
The evaluation addresses three questions: whether a shared model supports
both native tasks; whether attribute information transfers when annotation
coverage changes; and how much the final classification design contributes.
The current results answer the first at the level of a joint operating
point. The latter two require the specified ablations.

\subsection{Protocol and Comparison Sources}
\label{sec:protocol}
The joint model uses full BEATs fine-tuning, batch size 32, Adam with
learning rate $5\times10^{-5}$ and weight decay $10^{-6}$, a 50-epoch cosine
schedule with minimum learning-rate ratio 0.001, and parameter EMA 0.5.
Each epoch has 326 updates, divided equally between the sources. SpecAugment,
mean-variance normalization, and HF auxiliary supervision are disabled.

Internal ICBHI validation uses the first fold of a shuffled five-fold
stratified patient-group split, controlled by the run seed. SPRSound's
6,656 official training events are divided into 5,219 fitting and 1,437
validation events, with 194 and 49 patient groups. These data set attribute
thresholds. In the existing runs, checkpoint selection and patience-10 early
stopping instead use ICBHI official-test Score. The formal seeds 0, 1, and
fresh 42 select epochs 15, 11, and 17; SPRSound official inter is evaluated
once per selected checkpoint. The resulting mean and sample standard
deviation describe a test-selected benchmark. New matched comparisons will
use a common validation-based selection protocol, with its split, criterion,
and training budget fixed before execution.

For ICBHI, $Sp$ is Normal recall and $Se$ is the fraction of abnormal cycles
assigned to their correct abnormal subclass. Its Score is $(Sp+Se)/2$;
confusing Crackle with Wheeze therefore reduces $Se$. For SPRSound Task1-1,
$Se$ and $Sp$ are Adventitious and Normal recall. Its official Score is
$(AS+HS)/2$, where $AS=(Se+Sp)/2$ and $HS=2SeSp/(Se+Sp)$.

\subsection{Native-Task Performance with a Shared Model}
The joint model obtains ICBHI Score $61.17\pm0.31\%$ and SPRSound official
Score $90.70\pm0.34\%$. Its ICBHI $Sp$ and $Se$ are $74.98\pm5.69\%$ and
$47.35\pm5.35\%$. Class recall is $60.20\pm8.48\%$ for Crackle,
$29.61\pm2.08\%$ for Wheeze, and $36.83\pm11.37\%$ for Both.
SPRSound Normal and Adventitious recall are $84.29\pm1.36\%$ and
$97.60\pm1.21\%$, respectively. Thus the shared model supplies both
requested outputs, with uneven recognition of the fine ICBHI categories.
The small ICBHI Score variation does not imply equally stable class behavior.

The frozen BEATs references obtain 51.37\% on ICBHI and 92.00\% on SPRSound,
whereas the single-source PAFA checkpoint reaches 64.14\% on ICBHI.
These different reference points do not establish a sharing gain or cost:
they also differ in fine-tuning, inputs, losses, and selection. A matched
joint-versus-single-source comparison is needed to assess performance
retention and the value of adding another source's supervision.

\subsection{Attribute Reuse under Annotation Changes}
\label{sec:attribute_study}
To test what shared supervision contributes, the proposed annotation study
keeps the joint audio and architecture fixed while withholding SPRSound C/W training
targets, retaining its native abnormality supervision. Evaluation then asks
whether ICBHI attribute supervision supports C/W recognition on compatible
SPRSound labels, alongside its native binary task. Source-only attribute
learning and the complete-label joint model provide references for transfer
and available supervision, respectively. Attribute metrics use only samples
whose annotations support the corresponding target.

This setting varies the information available for training, rather than the
native evaluation labels. The target source still participates in coarse-task
training. To isolate attribute information, the weights of retained losses
must remain fixed: deleting C/W from the active-node average in
(\ref{eq:classification_loss}) would otherwise also increase the relative
weight of abnormality supervision. The exact validation-based selection rule
and attribute evaluation protocol remain to be fixed for these runs.

\subsection{Separating Classification and Supervision Effects}
\label{sec:design_study}
Two comparisons address the two-level design. First, a shared encoder with
native ICBHI-four-class and SPRSound-binary heads should have access to the
same fine attribute annotations, for example through matched auxiliary
attribute supervision. Comparing this model with the shared-attribute model
assesses the classification interface at matched information content. A
binary-only SPRSound head without attribute supervision would change both
the available information and the classification design.

Second, fixing the shared model's attribute scores allows a direct readout
comparison. C/W-only four-class conversion maps two negative attributes to
Normal, two positive attributes to Both, and a single positive attribute to
its class. Comparing this rule with the abnormality-gated rule isolates
changes in the final decisions. Both rules require validation-based threshold
selection. Since the encoder and scores stay fixed, this comparison cannot
be interpreted as a change in learned acoustic features.

\subsection{Scope of Additional Dataset Evidence}
Existing HF Lung and KAUH evaluations use fixed selected checkpoints and
compatible post-hoc readouts\cite{hsu2021hflung,fraiwan2021kauh}. The baseline
report instead uses HF temporal multi-label prediction and KAUH recording-level
nine-class classification. These are different tasks. They motivate broader
study but do not demonstrate that the present three-attribute model restores
those datasets' full native targets. The main argument therefore remains
centered on the two specified native tasks and the annotation/readout
comparisons above.
